# How this could pay for itself

Bar Vault is free, and what it does now stays free. This page is the honest version of how a free, private tool might earn, what would have to be true, and what I do not know.

No market sizes and no revenue figures. I have not measured a market, so I will not invent one.

## Who it's for

Adults who keep a home bar and want a record of it on their own phone: what is on the shelf, what each peg cost, what is low. The moment it is for is standing in a shop, wondering what is nearly empty at home. The privacy is the point. Nothing is typed into a stranger's website.

## What stays free

Everything in the app: the shelf, the numbers, the restock list, the exports, the locked backup. If the basics sat behind a login, nobody would pick it over a spreadsheet.

## The only plausible path: a bar sheet pack

A small pack for people who like paper next to the phone:

- printable shelf cards, one per bottle, with the fields the app uses (size, paid, peg size, low line)
- a blank CSV with the same columns as the app's whole-bar export, so it opens in a spreadsheet
- a one-page guide to filling a card by hand and typing it in

It would be sold as a download on a storefront. The app does not change and does not nag. The pack is for people who want the cards, and the app works fine without it.

**What has to be true:** enough people want a paper version of what is on their shelf to pay for a download, and the app is how they find it. I have no evidence yet that they do.

**Cost to deliver:** a few days of layout and a storefront listing. Hosting stays free, there is no server, and there is no price feed to pay for.

## What would be dishonest

- **A price feed.** The app never fetches or guesses a price, and it says so on the bottle form, Stats and About. Adding one would break the thing it promises.
- **Affiliate links to liquor stores.** A link that pays me when you buy turns a ledger into a shop window.
- **Anything that nudges consumption.** No streaks, no targets, no alerts that push you to restock. The app records what you own and gives no drinking advice.
- **Selling or reading the data.** The app collects none, so there is nothing to sell, and the page's policy blocks every connection.

## One cheap demand test

1. Post the live link with a screenshot of the invented sample bar.
2. Ask one question: "Would you print a shelf card for each bottle, and what would it need on it?"
3. Read the answers. Count replies that ask for the cards and replies that say a spreadsheet is enough. The app has no tracking and that is not changing, so I count at the door, not inside the room.

**Go:** people ask for the cards unprompted, and name fields I had not thought of. Make the first pack and offer it to those people.
**No-go:** the replies say the phone is enough. The app is fine and the pack is the wrong offer, so I leave the code alone.
**In between:** ask a different audience before building anything.

## What I don't know yet

- Whether anyone wants paper at all.
- Whether people keep a backup habit. If they do not, a clearer reminder matters more than any paid pack, and it is free.
- Whether an Alexa, Google Home or Apple Shortcuts routine is something people will set up by hand.

## Ground rules

- No ads and no selling data.
- Anything paid has a refund button.
- Not affiliated with any liquor brand or store.
- Not financial advice. Value left is the price paid, scaled by what is left.
